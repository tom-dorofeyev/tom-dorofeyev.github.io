# Journalistic and media writing

Choose structure according to purpose. Genre structures are editorial conventions,
not universal formulas. Accuracy, honest attribution, and clear distinctions
between fact, interpretation, and opinion apply across genres.

## Establish the assignment

Identify the audience, publication context, central question or angle, genre,
and available source material. Respect the requested voice, length, and scope.
Infer reasonable defaults; ask when missing information would materially change
the article. Do not import the structure of existing articles as a requirement.

When editing, preserve the author's meaning and voice. Do not turn opinion into
purported neutral reporting or insert a position the author has not taken.
For mixed genres, make transitions between reporting and commentary clear.

## Choose the structure

### News report

- Lead with the most consequential verified development. Assess newsworthiness
  through audience relevance, impact, timeliness, and public interest.
- Address the relevant **who, what, when, where, why, and how** early. Do not
  force all six into one sentence or supply a cause that remains unknown.
- Use the **inverted pyramid**: essential facts, supporting detail and source
  statements, then background. The essential story should stand on its own.
- Keep the reporter's personal opinion out of factual reporting. Attribute
  allegations and disputed claims precisely and include relevant responses.

### Explanatory or informational article

- Establish the question and why it matters, then explain the answer logically.
  Define unfamiliar terms when readers first need them.
- Use evidence, concrete examples, and useful comparisons. Distinguish
  illustrative scenarios from observed events.
- Explain limitations and implications without overstating certainty or consensus.

### Opinion article or op-ed

- State a specific, debatable thesis and why it matters to readers.
- Develop reasons supported by evidence and reasoning. Personal conviction
  alone does not establish a factual claim.
- Address strong, relevant counterarguments fairly. Do not invent weak opposing
  positions or require a counterargument section in every short piece.
- End with a consequence, recommendation, or judgment that follows from the argument.

### Editorial

- Use an argument structure like an op-ed, but express a publication's official
  position only when that position has been supplied or authorized.
- Connect the issue, institutional position, evidence, and proposed action.
  Do not present a personal viewpoint as an institution's policy.

### Feature article

- Choose a narrative or thematic structure that serves the reporting. Any opening
  scene, anecdote, person, or striking detail must come from available evidence.
- Use a **nut graf** where useful: a paragraph explaining the central subject,
  its significance, and what the story will explore, usually soon after the opening.
- Develop the piece through reported detail, context, and relevant voices.
  End meaningfully without manufacturing resolution.

### Analysis

- Explain what a development means through verified reporting, context, and
  explicit reasoning. Separate established facts from interpretation.
- Weigh plausible explanations and conflicting evidence. Identify uncertainty
  and distinguish conditional forecasts from known outcomes.

### Review or criticism

- Identify the subject and context; evaluate it against clear, appropriate
  criteria using specific examples. Make the judgment follow from that evaluation.
- Separate description from assessment. Disclose relevant relationships or
  provided access. Do not imply firsthand use or attendance without it.

### How-to or service journalism

- State the reader's problem or desired outcome, then present usable guidance
  in the order the reader needs it.
- Include prerequisites, meaningful limitations, and likely pitfalls. Verify
  actionable details and distinguish tested instructions from untested suggestions.

## Apply shared editorial standards

- **Verify:** Check names, dates, numbers, quotations, and material factual claims.
  Prefer original documents, data, and firsthand reporting when available.
  Check time-sensitive claims against current sources.
- **Attribute:** Place attribution close to the claim. Link to supporting sources
  when the format permits; otherwise retain enough detail for verification.
  Outlets repeating one source do not constitute independent confirmation.
- **Never fabricate:** Do not invent sources, interviews, quotations, statistics,
  scenes, personal experiences, or reporting activity. Omit or qualify unsupported
  claims, request needed material, or clearly mark draft gaps. Do not present an
  incomplete draft as publication-ready.
- **Quote faithfully:** Preserve wording and context. Distinguish paraphrases
  from quotations; do not selectively quote to alter a source's meaning.
- **Be fair:** Represent evidence and positions accurately. When reporting
  permits, give people facing material criticism a reasonable opportunity to
  respond; never imply contact occurred when it did not. Weight claims according
  to evidence rather than manufacturing equal credibility or equal space.
- **Reason carefully:** Distinguish correlation from causation, examples from
  representative evidence, and assertions from demonstrated conclusions. Supply
  relevant time frames, denominators, and comparison context for statistics.
- **Be transparent:** Identify material uncertainty and relevant conflicts of
  interest or sponsorship. Correct demonstrated errors rather than hiding them.
- **Exercise care:** Avoid unnecessary identifying details and sensationalism,
  especially when covering vulnerable people. Balance public interest against
  foreseeable harm and respect agreed source confidentiality.
- **Credit others:** Attribute borrowed reporting and ideas. Paraphrasing still
  requires attribution; avoid plagiarism and excessive quotation.

## Edit for publication

Make the headline accurate and proportionate to the article. Ensure the opening
fulfills its promise and establishes the focus. Use concrete language, explain
necessary jargon, remove repetition, and give paragraphs a coherent purpose.
Use headings and transitions when helpful; do not impose a fixed paragraph count,
formulaic conclusion, or academic essay format.

Before delivery, check genre fit, structure, evidence, attribution, fairness,
reasoning, and consistency between headline and body. Conclusions must not outrun
the reporting. Check links, captions, and numerical consistency where present.
Report unresolved factual gaps or unavailable verification plainly. Deliver the
requested article or edit; add a separate editorial assessment only when requested
or necessary to explain material unresolved issues.
